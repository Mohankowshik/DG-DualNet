Why Baseline Detectors Fail Out-of-Domain

Standard CNN classifiers memorize high-frequency color artifacts and specific camera sensor fingerprints of individual datasets (e.g. ProGAN or FaceForensics++ DeepFakes). When a new generation model appears (e.g. Stable Diffusion or Midjourney), those specific spatial artifacts do not exist, leading to false negatives.

How DG-DualNet Solves Domain Drift

1. Dual-Stream Fusion: Simultaneously analyzes RGB spatial blending boundaries and 2D-FFT Fourier spectra.
2. Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL): Adversarially forces the shared representation to become invariant to the generator domain while remaining discriminative between Real and Fake.

Adversarial Gradient Reversal (GRL)

During forward propagation, the GRL acts as an identity mapping: f(x) = x.

During backpropagation, it reverses gradients by scaling them by -λ:
∂L / ∂x = -λ · ∂L / ∂y This causes the feature extractor to maximize domain confusion while minimizing fake detection loss, stripping generator-specific bias.

Frequency Domain Forensic Signatures

Generative models that rely on upsampling (transposed convolutions or nearest-neighbor) introduce periodic grid artifacts in the frequency domain that are invisible to the naked human eye in the spatial domain.

By computing the 2D Fast Fourier Transform and azimuthal radial profiles, DG-DualNet reliably detects deepfakes even when photorealistic image resolution is high.
